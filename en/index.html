The pipeline

From raw seed to certified bag

Each process inherits the lot from the previous one and leaves an immutable record. No step can be skipped — if a lot did not pass fine cleaning, it does not enter the pelleting drum.

What the plant does

Protect the embryo, standardise the lot, certify germination

A naked seed is fragile: fungi attack it, it dehydrates easily, and mechanical planters reject it. Pelleting addresses all three problems at once. The seed enters the rotating drum and exits wrapped in successive layers of polymer, fungicide and inert clay — a micro-environment that shields it from the outside world and gives it the exact geometry that planting machinery can pick up.

The success of that coating depends on each previous step having done its job. Poorly cleaned seed receives uneven layers. A lot without traceability cannot be audited when a client reports poor germination. The plant models the flow as a finite state machine: every process blocks the next until the previous one is validated.

The result is a certified bag whose code any auditor can scan and reconstruct forward and backward — from the base seed that originated it to the operators who handled it.

How this site is read

Three levels of depth on every process

Each process card is written for three overlapping audiences. The first layer — In simple — explains what happens and why it matters, in half a paragraph. The second — In detail — goes into how it is done and what is controlled. The third — Data — shows ranges, units, and operational limits. Whether you are a member of the public, a student, an auditor, or floor staff, you stop at your level and go deeper if you want.
