Pelleting and Drying

Seed is wrapped in successive layers of polymer, fungicide and clay inside a rotating drum. The operator monitors temperature, rotation speed and proportions. The system blocks drum entry if the seed did not pass fine cleaning. Immediately after, the wet pellet moves to the drying turbine to cure the coating.

Drum + turbine Floor operator ~70 min per lot Cannot be skipped

In simple

Seed enters the pelleting drum and exits wrapped in layers: a polymer base first, then fungicide, then clay that gives it size and weight. That protects the seed, gives it the geometry mechanical planters need, and increases its germination.

In detail

The drum rotates at a controlled speed on a tilted axis. While it spins, automatic dosing units apply the coating in layers: polymer for cohesion, fungicide against soil pathogens, inert clay for mass and sphericity. The operator monitors five critical variables on a touch screen — drum temperature, rotation speed, exposure time, polymer/seed ratio, and mixing moisture. Capture is redundant: if the temperature sensor fails, the system blocks the lot. The output is wet crude pellets that move to drying.

Why this step is the heart of the pipeline

Pelleting concentrates the value investment: the polymer, the fungicide and the clay account for the bulk of the per-lot unit cost. It is also the step where any error becomes irreversible — excess temperature degrades embryo viability; a miscalibrated ratio produces uneven pellets. That is why the system does not allow a lot to enter the drum without the fine-cleaning step properly recorded. It is the plant's first algorithmic state-transition rule.

Phase 04.2

Drying

The wet pellets from the drum pass through the turbine. Controlled heat cures the polymer and hardens the coating. Too much heat damages the seed; too little leaves soft pellets that break in the bag.

Drying turbine Turbine operator ~30 min per lot Thermal

In simple

The pellet comes out of the drum wet. The turbine pulls the water out and hardens the polymer layer until the pellet feels firm to the touch.

In detail

The turbine runs a controlled temperature curve in three stages: an initial ramp to evaporate free water, a polymerisation plateau where the coating crosslinks, and a controlled descent so the seed does not suffer thermal shock. Ambient humidity also matters — dry days shorten residence time, humid days lengthen it. The system logs both sides of the equation to keep the balance.
